People and Culture · fusion structure

How we organize our content

A fusion approach: the topical folder structure the team designed, with two things layered on top. Folders to navigate · access levels to secure · columns to find — same content, three jobs.

1 · The three access levels — who can see it

Generalthe open shelf

Who can access

Everyone in People & Culture

Lives here: policies, templates, procedures, benefits & wellness info, general communications.

Restrictedthe locked cabinet

Who can access

A few People & Culture administrators

Lives here: personnel files, offer letters, contracts, compensation, SINs & other personal information.

Confidentialthe sealed vault

Who can access

The manager + assigned handler only

Lives here: active investigations, grievances, disciplinary matters, accommodation & medical records.

The levels aren't new folders. They're a property you paint onto the structure the team designed — most branches are General, a few are Restricted, and the most sensitive need Confidential. Here's how they land on our actual tree.

⚠ Missing today: a Confidential home. Active investigations, grievances and disciplinary matters have no place in this tree yet — and they can't sit in a browsable subfolder. They need their own Confidential library, locked to the manager + assigned handler. Worth deciding before go-live.

Lifecycle: folders hold roughly the last 5 years; older content moves to an Archive. SharePoint file-level auto-archiving (July 2026) may handle this automatically.

One more dial: depth. The team kept this tree shallow — about two to three levels — which is the sweet spot. The tool below shows what each extra level of nesting costs, so you can see why going deeper is where things went wrong before.
